This context defines the language used for the repository as a personal knowledge base.

## Knowledge

**Note**:
An authored unit of knowledge with a title, a primary kind, and a stable identity. A note may be useful before it is fully reviewed.

_Avoid_: Treating a note as the same thing as its source, its local copy, or its file path.

**Knowledge**:
Reusable concepts, methods, explanations, and reference material that can inform more than one project or moment.

**Project**:
Work organized around an objective, deliverables, and a lifecycle. Project notes describe the work and remain part of the project's knowledge.

**Journal note**:
A dated record of events, observations, decisions, or reflections. It is chronological by nature rather than reusable reference knowledge.

**Inbox note**:
A captured note that has not yet been placed in its lasting knowledge or project context. It may be incomplete, ambiguous, or contain unresolved links.

**Canonical note**:
A note placed in its lasting knowledge, project, or journal context. Canonical describes the note's location and role, not whether the note is stable or correct.

## Lifecycle

**Draft**:
A note that is still open to correction, organization, or review. A draft may be valuable, but it is not presented as fully endorsed.

**Stable**:
A note that a human has reviewed and considers fit for dependable reuse. Stable does not mean that the note can never be revised.

**Deprecated**:
A note retained for historical or navigational reasons but no longer preferred for current use.

**Verification**:
A human's explicit review of a note's content and relationships. Verification is different from authorship and from the existence of a source.

**Provenance**:
The record of who generated or verified a note and when. Provenance describes the history of a note; it does not by itself establish the truth of every claim.

## Classification

**Type**:
The one primary kind of a note, such as concept, method, decision, project, or journal.

**Tag**:
A secondary, controlled classification that adds a cross-cutting subject or characteristic without replacing the note's type.

**ID**:
The stable identity of a note or source. An ID is not a title, a path, or a summary, and should remain unchanged when only the title changes.

## Sources and Links

**Source**:
An external work or resource referenced by a note. A source can support a note, but referencing a source does not imply that every claim in the note has been checked against it.

**Source cache**:
A disposable local copy of a source used when the source itself is needed. The cache is not the canonical knowledge and may be absent on another platform.

**Wikilink**:
An internal reference from one note to another note. A wikilink expresses a navigational or conceptual relationship; it is not automatically an endorsement of the linked note.

**Index**:
A concise navigational note for a folder. It summarizes the folder and points to the notes that belong directly to it.

**Context**:
A scoped glossary that defines terms and language conventions. A context clarifies meaning; it does not replace a note, a source, or a design decision.
